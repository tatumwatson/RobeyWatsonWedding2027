Looking for things to do while you’re here?

Heber sits in the middle of Arizona’s high country, and plenty of fun is within a few hours’ drive. Here are some ideas for the extra time. Drive times are approximate, so check a map and the current hours before you go.

Close to the lodge

  • The Rim Lakes. About 25 to 30 miles west on Highway 260, a chain of pine-lined lakes along the Mogollon Rim. Willow Springs Lake and Woods Canyon Lake are the most popular for trout fishing and kayaking, and Woods Canyon rents boats on site. Anglers need an Arizona fishing license.
  • Rim Lakes Vista Trail. A short hike with wide views of the Rim and wildflowers in season. The Rim is lightning-prone, so head back if storms build.
  • The Rim Road. A 45-mile scenic drive along the top of the Mogollon Rim (Forest Road 300) with views of the forest far below.
  • Show Low and Fool Hollow Lake. About 35 to 40 miles southeast. Stroll the Deuce of Clubs main street, named for a legendary card game, browse the quirky Show Low Historical Museum, then paddle or fish at Fool Hollow Lake.
  • Pinetop-Lakeside. About 12 miles south of Show Low, with tall ponderosa pines, Woodland Lake Park, and Pinetop Brewing Company, a Belgian-style brewery.

Within ~2 hours

  • Payson and Tonto Natural Bridge State Park. Payson is about 55 miles southwest, and the park is roughly 10 miles beyond on Highway 87. The bridge is believed to be the largest natural travertine bridge in the world, rising 183 feet over a 400-foot tunnel. The trails are short and steep, and swimming under the bridge is not allowed.
  • Pine and Strawberry. Two small towns north of Payson. Strawberry’s log schoolhouse, built in 1885, is billed as the oldest standing schoolhouse in Arizona.
  • Petrified Forest National Park and Holbrook. Roughly an hour and a half away. Take Highway 377 north to Holbrook, then Highway 180 south to the park. You’ll find petrified wood, Painted Desert views, and a 28-mile park road. Holbrook was Hashknife cowboy country, and the Navajo County Museum in the 1898 courthouse still has its old jail. The Wigwam Motel on Route 66 is a fun photo stop.
  • Winslow, Meteor Crater, and Homolovi. Roughly two hours west on I-40, so plan most of a day. Meteor Crater marks an impact from roughly 50,000 years ago and has a visitor center with rim trails. Winslow’s “Standin’ on the Corner” park honors the Eagles song, and Homolovi State Park preserves a pueblo village occupied about 700 years ago by ancestors of the Hopi.

Farther afield

  • Flagstaff. About two hours and fifteen minutes west on I-40. This mountain college town has a walkable downtown, local breweries, and Lowell Observatory, which offers guided tours and evening telescope viewing. Winslow and Meteor Crater are along the route if you want to stop.
  • Grand Canyon South Rim. Roughly three and a half to four hours away, so we recommend making it an overnight trip. Many travelers stay in Flagstaff or Williams, and Flagstaff is about 90 minutes from the South Rim entrance.
  • Sedona and the Verde Valley. About three hours away, and best as a long day or an overnight. Sedona is known for its red rock scenery. Nearby, Montezuma Castle preserves Sinagua cliff dwellings, Tuzigoot is a 110-room pueblo ruin, and Jerome is a former copper-mining boomtown with big valley views.
  • Canyon de Chelly and Hubbell Trading Post. On the Navajo Nation, roughly three to three and a half hours away, so plan a very long day or an overnight. Canyon de Chelly’s South Rim Drive runs 16 miles to seven overlooks, including Spider Rock, an 800-foot spire. Visiting the canyon floor requires a Navajo guide. Hubbell Trading Post in Ganado, the oldest continuously operating trading post on the Navajo Nation, is a free and fascinating stop on the way. The Navajo Nation observes daylight saving time, so its clocks run an hour ahead of the rest of Arizona.

A little Heber history

Heber began in the winter of 1882, when settlers from drought-weary communities along the Little Colorado River went looking for better farmland in the pines. By spring 1883 they had built cabins and planted grain, and the town was named for a Latter-day Saint leader named Heber, though accounts disagree on which one. Early residents farmed potatoes and corn, shared the range with the Hashknife cowboys of the Aztec Land and Cattle Company, and waited on a weekly buckboard for the mail from Holbrook. Just south, the neighboring settlement of Wilford was abandoned by 1926 after years of overgrazing. By the 1930s Heber was a logging town, with horses hauling timber until 1965, and next door Overgaard grew up around a sawmill in 1936. Today the two towns are known together as Heber-Overgaard, and they still go all out for the Fourth of July, with a parade that has drawn crowds of up to 20,000.

A few local legends add some mystery. In 1975, a logger working in the nearby forest vanished for five days and resurfaced in Heber claiming a UFO abduction, a story that inspired the 1993 film Fire in the Sky. The Heber area is also known for tales of the Mogollon Monster, Arizona’s answer to Bigfoot, said to roam the Mogollon Rim. Whichever legends you believe, we think it’s the perfect place to gather.
